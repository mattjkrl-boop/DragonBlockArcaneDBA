# Complex Effects Standard

Binding for the DBA complex effects redo. Written at dba d74f13a, Nov 4, 2026. This standard supersedes the no-shapes pass presentation rule (audits/NO-SHAPES-STANDARD.md) wherever the two conflict. Themes, colors, damage, cooldowns, costs, timing, mechanics, ownership, and verification rules from SPECTACLE-STANDARD.md, BESPOKE-STANDARD.md, and the no-shapes standard still govern wherever they do not conflict with this document.

## Why this pass exists

The no-shapes pass converted every effect to material particle emission only. In game, that reads as soft round glowing particles with no structured pieces. Carter's verdict, verbatim in substance: "ur using just round particles, I want complex pieces, complex shapes and complex particles, all u did is make everything glowing particles."

The target is the confirmed reference picture (a trident slam): one effect, many different things in it at once. Forked lightning with sub-branches. Sharp rock shards and fractured ground plates at many sizes. Flame tongues with torn edges. Smoke wisps curling between the pieces. Small embers and sparks as filler, never as the body. Complexity comes from layered composition, not from any single technique.

This is not a revert. BespokeGeometry and LayeredRender stay deleted; they read as plain shapes and Carter rejected that too. The new toolkit in this standard clears a higher bar, argued per builder below.

## The layered composition rule

Every significant effect is a composition of up to three layers:

1. **Pieces layer (authored geometry).** ComplexGeometry builders (client/render/ki/ComplexGeometry.java): branched lightning, irregular shard meshes, fractured plate fans, crack fans, tendrils and filaments, flame tongues. Seeded per piece, irregular silhouettes, detailed treatment (below). Energy and fire pieces render through the DbaShaders dissolve and fresnel pattern; solid fragment pieces render through the plain ki type with faceted vertex shading.
2. **Complex particle layer (irregular sprites).** The five complex sprite types registered in this pass: SHARD_CHIP, SPARK_STREAK, WISP_CURL, EMBER_FRAGMENT, ASH_FLAKE. Their silhouettes are fragments, streaks, curls, and flakes, generated by tools/gen_complex_assets.py. They are emitted through the existing particle system (MaterialEmitters.Preset.fixed or direct addParticle), so no owner builds a private emitter.
3. **Material filler layer (existing system, unchanged).** DbaMaterialOptions materials through MaterialEmitters and DbaMaterialParticle: plasma, smoke, vapor, dust, mist, soul, darkness, glass dust, flame, ember, ash. This layer is atmosphere and fill: the glow between pieces, the smoke behind them, the dust under them. A soft round glow sprite is allowed here as minor filler only.

Hard composition rules:

- No significant effect is particles-only. Every class M and class L effect (classes below) carries a pieces layer.
- No effect body is made of round glow sprites. PLASMA material emission may never be the sole or dominant layer of a class M or L effect; it is filler around pieces.
- No plain primitives, ever: no smooth boxes, cylinders, discs, domes, spheres, helixes, or clean ribbons, and no flat single-color surfaces. KiRenderHelper.drawColoredBox, drawCylinder, drawDisc, drawSphere, and drawHelix have zero call sites today and keep zero.
- Pieces are seeded. The same seed draws the same piece on every client; seeds come from the entity's synced state, exactly as the no-shapes pass required for emission.
- Physical objects stay physical: weapon models, detailed projectile models, debris block models, blocks, mobs, races, dragon balls, the dread eye sprites. Where an effect throws real rock at scale, prefer real DebrisEntity chunks (DebrisSpawner) for the big pieces and use mesh shards for the fast near-field fragments debris entities cannot time. The audit names which effects already have a DebrisSpawner field.

## Effect classes and required layers

- **Class S (small):** status effect auras, marks, hit feedback, dodge sparks, small bursts. Required: complex particle layer matched to theme. Pieces optional. Filler as today.
- **Class M (standard ability):** slashes, waves, projectiles, charges, stances, single strikes. Required: pieces layer (at least one ComplexGeometry builder), complex particle layer, filler.
- **Class L (signature):** slams, shockwaves at full size, domains and fields, beams, storms, ultimates, form auras, the shared Bolt and SwingTrail systems. Required: pieces layer at full budget (two builder types, or one builder type used at full segment and count budget), complex particle layer, filler, and shader treatment on every energy or fire piece.

## The complexity bar, per builder

Each builder below states why it will not read as a plain shape or a round blob. Phase 2 owners use these builders as shipped; an owner who needs a variant extends the shared toolkit through the merge, never a private copy.

- **branchedLightning.** Trunk built by midpoint displacement at 3 to 5 levels (9 to 33 points), amplitude 0.16 of length decaying 0.52 per level, so the path is jagged at three scales at once. Plus 2 to 6 seeded forks that leave the trunk at 25 to 55 degrees, plus 0 to 2 sub-branches per fork: three branch orders, like the reference picture and unlike the old branch builder (one displaced polyline, no forks, uniform width). Every polyline tapers along its length and jitters width per segment (0.8 to 1.25), and is drawn twice: a wide dim sheath pass and a narrow near-white core pass, each as crossed ribbon pairs so it reads from every angle. Vertex alpha runs through the shader life remap, so the bolt erodes and flickers as a material, not a lit tube.
- **shard and shardBurst.** A shard is a closed irregular spindle: three control rings of 5, 6, and 5 vertices plus root and tip, each ring smooth-interpolated at 8 steps around its band, every control vertex displaced radially by seeded noise (0.72 to 1.27 of nominal), ring counts and angular offsets all seeded, so no two shards share a silhouette and none is a cone or a pyramid. One shard emits 48 triangles, 288 vertices double-sided. Faces carry per-face facet brightness (0.68 to 1.18 in seeded steps, at least three distinct levels per shard) over a root-to-tip color gradient: it reads as a fractured chunk of material, not a smooth solid. shardBurst mixes three size classes in one call (about 55 percent chips at 0.3 to 0.55 unit, 30 percent shards at 0.55 to 1.05, 15 percent chunks at 1.05 to 1.6), each with its own direction, roll, and length. A flat triangle fan (the old shardFan) fails every one of these tests.
- **fracturePlates.** Six to twelve ground plates radiating from a center, angular spans jittered, about 15 percent of plates missing entirely (gaps, not a fan). Each plate is a slab with real thickness: a top face split around a raised or sunken center vertex (nine vertices, no flat quad), tilted corners, and skirts down to the ground on three sides. Facet brightness steps and a hot-to-dark radial gradient. It reads as ground that broke and lifted, especially over the real craters the destruction pass now leaves.
- **crackFan.** Seven to eleven wandering cracks (the old crackStar had six to ten straight-ish runs). Each crack walks 4 to 6 segments with seeded angle wander, width that pinches at seeded points instead of tapering smoothly, and a 35 percent chance of one fork. Color runs hot at the center to the theme color at the tips. Drawn additively through the shader, so cracks glow as energy in the ground, not as decals.
- **tendril and tendrilFan.** A filament on a quadratic bezier spine sampled at 12 to 24 points, with curl offsets in 3D (two perpendicular sine and noise fields, amplitude growing mid-spine), a width profile that swells and then tears: two seeded pinch windows where the width collapses to 15 percent, so the filament reads as strands that split and rejoin. Drawn as a crossed ribbon pair whose planes twist 1.5 to 3.5 radians along the length (parallel frames, not a flat ribbon facing one way). Samples the tendril canvas texture under the shader, so edges erode. The old tornRibbon was one flat jittered strip; this is a 3D coiling strand with a textured, dissolving edge.
- **flameTongues.** Three to nine tongues per call, each a six-segment vertical strip whose centerline sways with height (phase passed in by the caller, so tongues move), whose width swells and pinches by seeded noise, and whose tip vertices are pulled sideways and inward so the top edge is torn in the mesh itself, not only in the texture. Tongues alternate radial and tangential planes so the cluster never reads as one flat sheet. The flame canvas texture (torn silhouette, streaked core) under the additive shader gives the dissolve and fresnel rim; vertex colors run hot at the base to the theme color at the tip.

## Shader wiring (DbaShaders)

client/render/ki/DbaShaders.java was built in the no-shapes pass and adopted nowhere, because no geometry survived that pass. This pass is its reason to exist. Rules:

- Energy and fire pieces (lightning, tendrils, flame tongues, crack fans, energy shards) submit through ComplexGeometry.submitEnergy (additive) or submitVeil (alpha), or call DbaShaders.additiveType() and alphaType() directly with the same discipline. One call puts a piece on the shader; owners never assemble pipelines.
- Alpha is life. The shader erodes a surface as its vertex alpha falls. Owners pass honest peak alphas (0.85 to 1.0 for effect bodies, lower only for wisps and veils). The toolkit's shader-bound builders apply the life remap internally (peak alpha times envelope, boosted 1.45 and clamped), so a body sits intact through build and hold and dissolves only in the decay tail. An owner must never feed a shader piece a low constant alpha to fake faintness; use color for faintness.
- Solid pieces (shard meshes, fracture plates) submit through ComplexGeometry.submitSolid (the plain ki render type): their read is faceted vertex shading, and the fresnel rim would only soften them.
- The fallback posture of DbaShaders stands: if the reflective pipeline chain fails on a future version, it logs once and degrades to the ki type. Pieces still draw; they lose the dissolve, not the effect.

## Textures and sprites

- Generator of record for this pass: tools/gen_complex_assets.py. It writes the five complex sprite sets (128 px, eight frames) into textures/particle with their particles/*.json, and two geometry canvas textures into textures/entity/fx: flame_tongue.png and tendril.png (128 px, torn silhouettes with streaked cores, white intensity so vertex color carries theme).
- Sprite silhouette rules: shard chips are hard-edged polygons with at least two facet shades; spark streaks are tapered elongated slivers with a bright core line; wisp curls are spiral comma bands; ember fragments are dark crust polygons around a bright cracked core; ash flakes are small multi-lobe clusters. No set may be a radial-gradient disc. Frames vary the silhouette itself (rotation, polygon, facet split), not only brightness.
- The five sprite types are physical-material sprites with fixed style colors (stone gray chips, white-gold streaks, pale curls, orange ember fragments, gray ash). Theme color comes from the pieces and filler layers; the sprites are the neutral physical debris every theme throws. Owners emit them with MaterialEmitters.Preset.fixed(DbaParticles.SHARD_CHIP, velocity, spread) and siblings, or MaterialEmitters.burst for one-shot events.
- Material flipbooks from the no-shapes pass stay as they are. Do not regenerate them in this pass.

## Performance budgets

Budgets are per effect instance at distScale 1.0, and scale down with FxEnvelope.distScale like everything else. A submit that exceeds its class budget is a merge defect.

| Surface | Budget |
|---|---|
| Total piece vertices per submit | S: 800, M: 2500, L: 6000 |
| branchedLightning | 130 path segments total (trunk plus forks plus sub-branches), 4200 vertices |
| shardBurst | 16 meshes per burst; at most 300 vertices per mesh (the shipped shard emits 288, counted from its 48 double-sided triangles). Class L compositions combining a burst with plates and cracks use 12 or fewer shards so the per-submit total holds. |
| fracturePlates | 12 plates, 1600 vertices |
| crackFan | 11 cracks, 900 vertices |
| tendril | 24 spine samples per tendril, 6 tendrils per fan |
| flameTongues | 9 tongues, 6 segments each |
| Complex sprite bursts | S: 12, M: 30, L: 60 per burst event |
| Complex sprite continuous emission | 40 per second per emitter |

## Merge gates for Phase 2

Phase 2 is judged by these gates, checked fresh at the merge, in addition to the standing gates (dual javac sweeps with zero new error kinds on both versions, all JSON parses, every texture reference resolves, git diff --check clean, added-lines em dash scan zero, themes and mechanics identical):

1. Every class M and class L effect in audits/complex-audit.md has at least one ComplexGeometry builder call in its renderer or visuals file, and at least one complex sprite type emitted. Class L effects meet the two-builder or full-budget rule.
2. No class M or L effect is particles-only, and none has PLASMA material emission as its dominant layer.
3. Zero calls to KiRenderHelper.drawColoredBox, drawCylinder, drawDisc, drawSphere, or drawHelix anywhere in effect code. BespokeGeometry and LayeredRender remain deleted and unreferenced.
4. Every energy or fire piece goes through submitEnergy, submitVeil, or a DbaShaders render type. Every solid piece goes through submitSolid or the ki type with facet shading.
5. No private geometry toolkit, private particle system, or private texture style in any owner file. Variants are proposed as shared toolkit extensions at the merge.
6. An owner whose conversion reads worse than the current state says so plainly in their report and does not ship it silently; the merge reverts that piece and records it.

## Scope

In scope: every effect surface in audits/complex-audit.md (the same 31 owner slots as the no-shapes pass). Presentation only: no damage number, cooldown, cost, timing, mechanic, or synced data field changes. The destruction wiring from d74f13a (PermanentDestruction, lava settling, retired GroundScar) is not touched. Split source sets stay clean: ComplexGeometry and the submit helpers are client side; DbaParticles stays common and references no client classes.
